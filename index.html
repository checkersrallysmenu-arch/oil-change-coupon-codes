How oil change coupons actually work

Most oil change shops are franchises. The brand sets national offers, but local franchise groups publish their own coupons too, which is why the same chain can have a $30 off deal in Florida and a 50% off deal in Oregon at the same time. The coupon that counts is the one your store's group is running, so always match the offer to your location before you go.

Deals rotate roughly every month. A coupon that expired yesterday is usually replaced by a fresh one within days, so if the offer you want just ended, check the brand's official coupon page again before paying full price. Every deal on this site lists its expiry date, and the pages are refreshed as new offers land.

Before you go: a 30 second checklist

  • Check the expiry date on the coupon itself, not on a screenshot someone sent you last month.
  • Match the offer to your store. Franchise groups honor different coupons, sometimes in the next town over.
  • Know your oil type and how many quarts your engine takes. Anything past the included 5 quarts is charged extra.
  • Show the coupon at check in, before the service starts. Mentioning it afterwards is how most discounts get lost.
  • Read the invoice before you pay. The discount should appear as its own line.

Quick questions

Why does the same chain have different coupons in different states?
Franchise groups publish their own coupons on top of the national offers, so a store in Texas and a store in Oregon can be running completely different deals on the same day.

Do I need to print the coupon?
Usually not, most stores accept the coupon on your phone. If the coupon itself says to print it, print it, because the terms printed on the coupon are what the store follows.

How often do these deals change?
Roughly every month. Coupons carry printed expiry dates, and expired ones are normally replaced within days, which is why every deal on this site shows its date.

Can I use two coupons together?
Almost never, chains allow one offer per service. Pick the bigger saving. A prepaid card style offer is the usual exception, because the oil brand pays for it rather than the store.

Why was my bill higher than the coupon price?
Extra quarts past the included 5, a premium filter, diesel oil, and taxes and shop fees are the usual reasons. Ask the advisor to price the full job before the work starts.
